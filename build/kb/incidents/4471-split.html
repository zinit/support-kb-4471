The three problems

parser — the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a date with a day of 12 or less flips: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” It comes in by app, email and form, from every tenant. This is the bug #4471 named.

hint — the booking-v3 form tells people to type month first. Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY, people type what it says, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The date flips the same way, but the parser is not involved: it only comes in by form, and only from tenants that run booking-v3. Changing the parser leaves it as it is; the hint has to change.

shift — the date moves by one day. “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” Swapping day and month cannot move a date by one day, so these are not the parser, even though their tenants write the day first. The pattern — a weekday off, a different day per time zone, “only June”, an invite that arrived before the booking — points at time-zone or daylight-saving handling. The desk had already read them as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) and still files them by tenant, so the table counts them under parser too, the way the desk’s own view does; they still need their own fix.

How they were told apart

  • Counted by id, not by row: the ticket export pages overlap.
  • Not by intake’s tag: it is a guess from the reporter’s browser, and it is wrong for many of them.
  • Not by subject: decision 0001 merges on subject alone, and every problem here shares the same dozen subjects. That is how three problems became one ticket.
  • By what the report says and where it comes from: the tenant’s locale and booking form (causes.ts), the channel, and the body.
